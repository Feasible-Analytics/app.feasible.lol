Most of these are what you would expect. The four at the bottom are the ones that surprise people, and they surprise people in every analytics product — we would rather write them down than let you discover them in a meeting.

The metrics

  • Pageviews — one per page load, plus one per client-side route change.
  • Visitors — distinct people in the selected period, counted by the daily visitor identifier described in privacy.
  • Visits (sessions) — a run of activity that ends after 30 minutes of silence, or at midnight in the site's timezone.
  • Views per visit — pageviews divided by visits.
  • Bounce rate — the share of visits with exactly one pageview and no interaction. An engagement ping is not an interaction.
  • Visit duration — last activity minus first activity within a visit. A single-page visit has a duration only if the tab reported time on page.
  • Time on page — measured by the script while the tab is visible, so a page left open in a background tab does not accumulate hours.
  • Scroll depth — the furthest point reached, as a percentage of the document height at the moment it was measured.

Four things that are not bugs

Visitors are not additive

The visitors for January plus the visitors for February is not the visitors for January-to-February, because somebody who came in both months is one person across the range and two people across the two months. This is true of every product that counts distinct people. Do not sum a visitors column.

Unique visitors can exceed pageviews

An event that is not a pageview — a custom event, an outbound click — can be the only thing a visitor sends. Filter to a segment made of those and you will see more visitors than pageviews, which is arithmetic rather than an error.

Attribution is frozen at the start of a visit

The source, campaign and channel are decided by the first event in a visit and then stamped onto every event in it. A visitor who arrives from a search, then clicks one of your own emails mid-visit, stays attributed to the search. This is what makes a report add up: if attribution changed mid-visit, the same visit would appear under two sources and every total on the page would be larger than reality.

Goals do not backfill

A goal starts counting from the moment you create it. It is not a retroactive query over what you already collected — the historic events were not evaluated against a rule that did not exist. Create the goal before the campaign, not after it.

What is not counted towards your bill

Engagement pings and pageview goals. A pageview goal is a saved view of pageviews you have already paid for, and an engagement ping is the script's own heartbeat. Billing either would be charging you for the same event twice. See the APIs for exactly which event names are which.
