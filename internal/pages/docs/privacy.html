The short version: no cookies, no persistent identifier, no cross-site tracking, no data sold or shared, and no IP address ever written to disk. The longer version below includes the part most products in this category leave out.

The visitor identifier is pseudonymous, not anonymous

We identify a returning visitor within a day by hashing their IP address, their user agent and the site's domain together with a secret salt. The salt is rotated every 24 hours and the old one is deleted, so the same person on the same device is a different identifier tomorrow, and the same person on two different sites is never the same identifier at all.

That is a genuinely strong design, and it is still pseudonymous under the GDPR rather than anonymous. While a salt exists, somebody holding both the salt and the stored hashes could brute-force the inputs back out — the space of IP addresses and user agents is small enough to search. Deleting the salt after 24 hours is what closes that door, and it is the entire basis of the claim, which is why we treat the salt table as being as sensitive as raw address logs and why it is encrypted at rest.

We say this plainly because the distinction is real and because a product that calls it "fully anonymous" is overstating what the maths gives you.

What we store

  • The page path, with every query parameter except seven acquisition ones stripped.
  • The referrer's host, the source and the channel.
  • Country, and region or city where the data allows.
  • Device type, browser, operating system and their versions.
  • Screen size bucket, and the browser's primary language.
  • Time on page and scroll depth, when the script measured them.
  • Any custom properties you chose to send.

What we never store

  • The IP address. It is used for geolocation and the identifier and then discarded, in the ingestion tier, before anything is written or forwarded. The type that crosses that boundary has nowhere to put one.
  • The raw user agent string.
  • Click identifiers. We record that one was present — which is how a paid click is told from an organic one — and never its value, because a click id is a unique per-click identifier.
  • Anything in a query string other than the seven acquisition parameters.

Cookies

The tracking script sets none and reads none. It uses no local storage for identification. Most legal advice concludes that no consent banner is required for this design, but we are not your lawyers and your regulator is not ours — the facts above are what you need to give to somebody who is.

Who processes your data

For the hosted service, Cloudmanic Labs, LLC is the processor and you are the controller. Our sub-processors are listed in the data processing addendum. We do not sell, rent or share visitor data, and there is no advertising business anywhere near this product.

Requests from a data subject

Because nothing we store identifies a person, an access or erasure request usually has nothing to match against — there is no row that says "this is Maria". If a custom property you sent does identify somebody, that property is yours and you can delete it. Write to us and we will help.

Deleting an account

Deletion removes the analytics database, the account records and the customer record with our payment provider. There is no soft delete and no recycle bin: keeping one would mean the deletion we spent ninety days warning you about was not real. See the timetable on the pricing page.

Where the data lives

On our own servers, in one SQLite database per account. There is no third-party data warehouse, no analytics-on-our-analytics, and no copy of your data anywhere we have not told you about.
