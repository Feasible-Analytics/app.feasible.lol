Availability. The counting engine described here is built and its behaviour is final, and the API routes exist with their final shapes. What is not wired up yet is the screen you would manage goals from, and the handlers behind /api/v1/sites/goals and the funnel tools — they answer 501 today, naming the feature rather than pretending the URL is wrong. This page describes how they count, so that when they arrive nothing about the numbers is a surprise. Everything else in these docs describes something you can use now.

Goals

A goal is one of exactly two things: a page somebody reached, or a custom event they sent. There is no third kind and there is not going to be one.

A pageview goal matches a path, optionally with wildcards: /checkout/thank-you, or /blog/**. * stays inside one path segment and ** crosses them, so /blog/* is the posts directly under /blog and /blog/** is everything beneath it. The pattern is anchored at both ends, so /pricing does not match /enterprise-pricing-guide. It costs nothing towards your monthly volume, because the pageview it matches was already counted.

A custom event goal matches an event you send by name:

feasible('Signup')
feasible('Purchase', { revenue: { amount: 49.00, currency: 'USD' } })

Custom events do count towards your monthly volume, because each one is an event we received and stored.

Either kind can be narrowed by up to three property constraints, and those are plain equality — plan = yearly, not plan contains y. If you find yourself wanting an operator there, the thing you want is a filter on the report, not a different goal.

Every site gets four goals for free

Because the tracker can already see these without you writing any code:

  • 404 — 404 pages
  • Outbound Link: Click — Outbound link clicks
  • File Download — File downloads
  • Form: Submit — Form submissions

They are created with the site rather than when the first matching event arrives, because a goal that springs into being with the traffic would start counting after the thing you were trying to measure. They stay hidden from the report until they have at least one conversion, so a site that never sends a 404 event does not carry an empty row forever.

Goals start when you create them

This is the single most common surprise, so it gets its own heading. A goal is evaluated as events arrive, not retroactively over your history — the historic events were never tested against a rule that did not exist. Creating a goal today gives you a number that starts at zero today.

A report over a range that begins before the goal did says so, and gives you the date it actually starts from, rather than quietly showing a smaller number. There is a worked example on the metrics page.

Creating the same goal twice is a no-op that hands you back the one you already had — including its original creation date. You cannot restart a goal's history by recreating it, and renaming one never moves it.

What a goal report shows

  • Unique conversions — once per visit, however many times it happened.
  • Total conversions — every matching event.
  • Converted visitors, and the conversion rate: converted visitors over all visitors in the period.
  • Revenue, average revenue and revenue per visitor, when the goal carries money.

Funnels

A funnel is an ordered list of goals — at least two steps and at most eight — and it counts visitors by how far they got.

Three rules follow from how it is walked, and all three matter:

  • A funnel is scoped to one visit. Somebody who signs up on Monday and buys on Thursday is two visits and completes nothing. Measure that with a pair of goals rather than a funnel.
  • Order is a setting, not a law. With strict order on, the steps have to happen in sequence and skipping one means leaving the funnel. With it off, a visit counts if it did all of them in any order. Pick deliberately: a checkout is strict, a "did they do all three of these things" is not.
  • One event advances at most one step. A wildcard goal and the exact page beneath it cannot both be satisfied by the same pageview, which is what stops a funnel completing itself on a single load.

Each step reports visitors, visits, drop-off against the step before it, and conversion rate against the first step. A funnel's measured window starts at the creation date of its newest goal, and it says so — otherwise adding a step to an old funnel would render as a cliff that never happened.

Journeys

For one page, a journey report answers "what happens either side of this". It lists the pages and the events that immediately preceded and immediately followed it within the same visit, up to twenty each way, with the page's own views and visitors alongside so every percentage has its denominator in the same answer.

Two things are deliberate. Visits that started or ended on the page appear as rows called (entry) and (exit) rather than being left out, because "most people leave here" is the most useful thing such a report can tell you. And trailing slashes are not normalised: /about/ and /about stay two rows, matching what Top Pages shows you.

Revenue

Revenue rides on a custom event, as an amount and an ISO 4217 currency:

feasible('Purchase', { revenue: { amount: 49.00, currency: 'USD' } })

It is stored in minor units — cents, pence — and never converted when it is written, so a change in exchange rates cannot rewrite a past quarter. Conversion happens at read time.

Three consequences worth knowing:

  • If a range holds more than one currency and you have not said which one to total in, the query is refused with a message naming the currencies it found. Adding numbers in different currencies and calling it a total is worse than an error.
  • Money we could not convert for want of a rate is reported as a warning naming how much was left out, rather than being silently dropped from the total.
  • Rounding happens once, on the total — not on every event and then again on the sum.

Revenue is credited to the source that started the visit, not to the last page before payment. Your payment provider therefore never appears in your sources report, even though visitors come back through it.
